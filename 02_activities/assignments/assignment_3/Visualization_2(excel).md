    > What software did you use to create your data visualization?

I used Excel's Pivot table for data visualization.

    > Who is your intended audience? 

The audience here is the logistics team and operations. This graph shows the top 10 most used bike hubs, and would help them develop strategies to ensure that there are enough bikes at each hub, and that every hub will have enough room for docking a bike.
    
    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 

This visualization uses the Pareto principle (80/20) rule. It uses a small number of stations which has the most amount of traffic. It shows how the system isn't evenly used and where the hubs of ridership happen.
    
    > What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 

I chose the Horizontal Bar Chart as that accomodates the long hub names. I also included the station numbers since the personnel would understand those numbers. I applied a Sort logic by descending values to show which station is the busiest. 
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 

Since Excel can easily change the cell values, I decided to keep the raw data and the pivot table/chart on separate tabs. Rather than filtering out the data on the raw data page, I used the top 10 value filter within the Pivot logic of the pivot table/chart. This ensures that the results are reproducible.
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  

I used standard colours - dark blue bars on a white background, and since there aren't extra colours with meaning, it ensures that people with colour blindness can see the data. 

    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  

    By highlighting the top 10 stations, this visualization similarly risks blindsighting the less utilized stations. If budget decisions are made solely on this chart, much of the budget will be allocated to the busier hubs and neglecting areas with less density that may need or desire more transit options. 
    
    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 

    I focused on Start Station Name and Trip ID. I then decided to include both the number and the station name to ensure it is readable both by the general population as well as the operations team. I excluded the end station as that deserves its own chart and convolutes the representation of this chart.
